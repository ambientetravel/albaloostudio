The route

The eastern shore of the Adriatic runs from the Venetian lagoon down the Croatian coast to Dubrovnik and on to the Bay of Kotor in Montenegro. Its islands and walled towns are close together, which suits smaller ships that can use ports and anchorages larger ones cannot.

Large cruise ships no longer sail through the centre of Venice: since 2021 they use terminals outside the historic city, such as Fusina, and the inventory names the port each line uses. Croatia joined Schengen in 2023, so an Adriatic cruise is a Schengen cruise even when it also calls in Montenegro or Albania.

Visa on this route

The rule follows the ports, not where you board: one call in a Schengen country puts the whole cruise under Schengen rules. Your residence visa does not change this; your passport does.

  • Schengen: Schengen rules for the whole cruise: a Schengen visa, unless your passport is Schengen visa-exempt. We confirm the visa position for your passport before you pay anything. (67 of 77 itineraries)
  • Schengen: Schengen rules for the whole cruise: a Schengen visa, unless your passport is Schengen visa-exempt. Türkiye: visa-free or e-visa for many passports, not all. We confirm the visa position for your passport before you pay anything. (10 of 77 itineraries)
