# The units are not characters yet, and every one of them is a man

*Art direction. Affects all 25 prompts in `ART-PROMPTS-UNITS.md`, which are
already generated — so this is a v2 pass, not a rewrite of shipped work.*

## The defect, stated plainly

Read the roster aloud: Persian Archer, Shield-bearer, Median Spearman, Kissian
Levy, Bactrian Archer, Caspian Skirmisher… **These are categories, not
characters.** They are Herodotus's army catalogue, which is why they are accurate
— and also why not one of them is anybody.

Compare the cards people actually love in the games you are pointing at. The Log.
The Miner. The Dart Goblin. Not one of those is a troop type; each is a
personality you can describe in four words. **"Persian Archer" cannot be
described in four words because there is no one there.**

And: **25 units, 25 men, one age.** No women, no youths, no elders. That was not
a decision anybody made — it fell out of using an army list as a roster — but it
is what shipped, and it is a real defect for an audience that is half girls.

## Part 1 — women in the units, without inventing anything

Three honest routes. The first is the strongest and costs nothing.

### Route A — the Saka horse-archer is a woman, and the graves say so
`saka-horse-archer` is already unit #8. Across the Eurasian steppe, excavation
has repeatedly turned up **women buried with weapons** — bows, arrowheads,
horse gear — and in examined weapon-burials the female share runs to roughly a
fifth. **This is physical evidence, not literature**, and it is the archaeology
sitting underneath Herodotus's Amazon stories.

The Saka were an Achaemenid contingent. **A woman on a Saka horse is not an
invention, it is a grave good.** Change one existing prompt and the roster has
its first woman with better evidence behind her than several of the men.

**Chorasmian and Sagartian riders** come from the same steppe world and can
follow.

### Route B — the camp, which is where the evidence actually is
The **Persepolis Fortification Tablets** — administrative clay, not chronicle —
record **women workers drawing rations, women supervising work gangs and drawing
more than the men under them, and extra rations for new mothers.** Two named
women, **Irdabama** and **Irtashduna** (Greek: Artystone, daughter of Cyrus and
wife of Darius), ran their own estates and **issued orders under their own
seals**.

Nobody has heard of Irdabama. She is attested in clay, in the administration of
the largest empire on earth, with her own seal. That is a codex entry and
possibly a support unit.

### Route C — the eras the game has not reached
The roster is Achaemenid because the built battle is Pasargadae. Sasanian,
Saffarid and Safavid rosters are all still unwritten, and each opens more.

**What I will not do:** make the Immortals mixed, or put a woman in the Lydian
phalanx. There is no evidence and the game's whole promise is that it does not
invent. **Apranik is what happens when you fill a gap with a wish** — that
argument is already written down in `DESIGN-WOMEN.md`, and it applies to our own
art exactly as much as to a poster.

## Part 2 — youths, which is your best idea on this page

> *"young persian boys and girls can be super interesting characters"*

Correct, and it is historically free. Ancient armies ran on people aged twelve to
fifteen: **grooms, water carriers, arrow-runners, drummers, shield-boys.** Not
one of them requires an invention, and every one of them is a face a
twelve-year-old recognises as their own.

**Three to add, mixed:**
- **آب‌رسان — the Water-carrier.** A girl of about twelve who runs between lines
  with a skin of water. Support: restores a squad that is about to break. The
  most beloved card in the game if the art is right.
- **تیرآور — the Arrow-runner.** A boy who gathers spent arrows under fire and
  brings them back. Refills an archer's shots.
- **the Groom.** Keeps the horses. Cavalry recovers between rounds.

And at the other end — **elders.** A grey-bearded veteran and an old woman of the
camp. **A card roster with one age band looks like a toy; one with four looks
like a people.**

## Part 3 — the appeal question, which is real, and the skin question, which I am declining

**On appeal you are completely right and I have under-served it.** Historical
accuracy has been doing all the work in these prompts and charm has been doing
none, and charm is what makes a child want the next card.

**On showing skin — no, and here are the four reasons, in order of how much they
should matter to you.**

1. **The audience is nine to fourteen.** That is the line, and I will not design
   sexualised characters for it.
2. **It would cost you the market you actually want.** This game's buyers are
   diaspora parents, and its best distribution is schools and community
   associations. **Sexualised character art pushes an App Store rating to 17+ and
   a Google Play rating to Mature**, and both of those close the parent and the
   classroom immediately. The tennis games you are describing are rated for
   adults and sold to adults.
3. **It is historically wrong in the same way "Arabian Gulf" is wrong.**
   Achaemenid and Sasanian dress was covering, for everyone, and cold-weather
   contingents doubly so. A bare-midriff Persian archer is a factual error, and
   it is exactly the error the whole project exists to avoid.
4. **It does not even work.** The most-loved cards in the games you are citing
   are a wooden log, a goblin with a blowpipe and a man with a shovel. Appeal in
   this genre comes from silhouette and personality. It has never come from skin.

**Here is what actually produces the thing you want:**

- **Silhouette first.** Recognisable as a black shape at 26px — the ledger
  constraint we already fixed and already measured. If two units share a
  silhouette, one of them is wrong.
- **One exaggerated feature each.** The Immortal's spear-butt apple. The camel
  rider's ridiculous saddle height. The Sagartian's coiled lasso, oversized.
  **One thing per card, pushed 30% past life.**
- **They look at you.** Eye contact turns a troop type into somebody. Not one of
  the current 25 does this.
- **An idle tell.** Two seconds of animation that says who they are — the
  water-carrier shifts her weight because the skin is heavy, the arrow-runner
  is already crouched to bolt.
- **Warmth over grimness.** These are children's cards. Dignified, not dour, and
  never snarling — which is already a rule in every prompt and should now be
  pushed the other way, into charm.
- **A name, not a category.** *Anahita of the Saka*, not *Saka Horse-archer*, with
  the contingent as the subtitle. **The contingent stays accurate; the character
  arrives on top of it.**

That last line is the whole fix. Nothing about the history has to give way for
these cards to become lovable — the history is the costume, and there is
currently nobody wearing it.
