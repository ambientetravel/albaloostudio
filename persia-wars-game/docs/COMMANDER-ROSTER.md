# The roster — nine commanders, nine ways to play

SETTLED at nine. Ardashir I is out — he duplicated Shapur's era, father and
son sixteen years apart — and Babak and Yaqub are in, filling the thousand-year
gap between Qadisiyya and the Safavids that the roster previously skipped.

**Honesty label, same as §10 claim 5.** The HISTORY below is sourced. The KITS
are gameplay abstractions built from that history — no Persian army ever had a
mechanic called "The Arrow Train". They are labelled reconstructed wherever they
appear in the Scrolls, exactly as the Upgrade and Doctrine names already are.

---

### 1 · CYRUS THE GREAT — the Median war · c. 550 BCE · **BUILT**
Won because other peoples were willing to fight alongside him, then did not
punish them afterwards.
- **Many Peoples** — every DIFFERENT kind of squad makes the whole line hold
  longer. Four of the same does nothing.
- **The Line Reforms** — your first lost squad gets back up once, at half.
- *Plays: wide. Rewards a mixed army.*

### 2 · DARIUS THE GREAT — the year of rebellions · 522–520 BCE
Nine kings and nineteen battles in one year, then carved his account of it into
a cliff in three languages, sixty metres up where nobody could reach it.
- **One After Another** — each round you WIN, your next squad arrives one rank
  higher. Losing resets it.
- **Cut Into The Cliff** — lock your current score advantage: the next round
  cannot be lost, only won or drawn.
- *Plays: streaks. Terrifying ahead, ordinary behind. The mirror of Cyrus.*

### 3 · SURENA — Carrhae · 53 BCE
Destroyed a Roman consular army with horse-archers and a camel train carrying
spare arrows, using feigned retreats. Executed by his own king within the year.
- **The Arrow Train** — your archers and horse-archers do not fall off; they hit
  harder the longer a round runs.
- **Feigned Flight** — your whole line withdraws and keeps shooting for a few
  seconds. Melee that chases is punished.
- *Plays: ranged attrition. Never wants to close. Historically exact.*

### 4 · SHAPUR I — three Roman emperors · 244–260 CE
Misiche, Barbalissos, and Edessa where the emperor Valerian was taken alive. All
three are attested by Shapur's OWN trilingual inscription. His Roman captives
built the bridge at Shushtar that still stands.
- **Roman Hands** — every enemy squad you break leaves something behind that
  strengthens your line.
- **The Bridge at Shushtar** — spend what you have taken: one squad is rebuilt
  to full.
- *Plays: grind and convert. Rewards a long, attritional match.*

### 5 · ROSTAM FARROKHZAD — Qadisiyya · c. 636 CE · **A DEFEAT**
**NOT the Rostam of the legends.** Two different men share this name and both
are in Ferdowsi. Rostam son of Zal — Rakhsh, the Seven Labours, the White Div —
is the hero of the Shahnameh's mythical age and appears in this game as SPELL
cards. Rostam Farrokhzad is a real Sasanian general of the Ispahbudhan house,
son of Farrokh Hormizd, attested in al-Tabari and in the Armenian history of
Sebeos. He is the commander. The codex must say this in the entry itself,
because a knowledgeable adult conflated them on first reading.

Four days on the Euphrates plain with the larger army, and the empire ended
anyway. Sources conflict sharply; the Arabic accounts are written later by the
winners.
- **The Fourth Day** — the further behind you are, the harder your line fights.
- **The Elephants** — deploy elephants that panic enemy cavalry outright.
- *Plays: the comeback kit. Best when losing, which is the point.*

### 6 · BABAK KHORRAMDIN — the Qaradagh · c. 816–837 CE
Twenty years holding a mountain against the Abbasid Caliphate from the fortress
at Badhdh, breaking army after army. Finally beaten by Afshin — an Iranian
prince of Ushrusana, which is the most interesting fact about the war and must
not be smoothed away.
- **Twenty Years** — every squad that SURVIVES a round hardens permanently. He
  wins by keeping the same men alive, not by replacing them.
- **Badhdh** — your line falls back to broken ground for a round; enemy cavalry
  and chariots are halved there.
- *Plays: persistence. The only kit that rewards protecting what you already have.*
- *Content note: his execution is the most famous fact about him and is not
  depicted. The codex states it without detail.*

### 7 · YAQUB IBN AL-LAYTH — Dayr al-Aqul · c. 861–879 CE
A coppersmith from Sistan — *saffar* — who came up through the ayyaran
brotherhoods, founded a dynasty, and marched on Baghdad. Remembered for
rejecting a poet's Arabic panegyric: *why should I say things I do not
understand?* The anecdote is from the Tarikh-e Sistan and is probably literary,
but the restoration of Persian at court is not.
- **The Coppersmith** — your LEVY squads fight far above their rank. You win
  with cheap troops, which is the inversion of every other kit here.
- **The Ayyaran** — one Levy squad is promoted on the spot to the round's cap.
- *Plays: the bottom of the ladder. Strongest where everyone else is weakest.*

### 8 · SHAH ISMAIL I — Chaldoran · 1514 CE · **A DEFEAT**
Safavid cavalry charged Ottoman artillery and janissary muskets. He was wounded,
lost, and never led an army again.
- **The Charge** — your cavalry hit far harder on the FIRST clash of a round,
  and no harder after it.
- **Qizilbash** — every cavalry squad advances at once, immediately.
- **Weakness, deliberately:** musketeers and artillery hurt him more than anyone.
- *Plays: all-in, first round. Teaches its own lesson by losing to gunpowder.*

### 9 · NADER SHAH — Karnal · 1739 CE
Beat a far larger Mughal army in about three hours and opened Delhi. Carried the
Peacock Throne home. Assassinated by his own officers eight years later.
- **Fewer and Faster** — while you have FEWER squads than the enemy, your whole
  line acts faster.
- **Three Hours** — end the round immediately; whoever leads on remaining health
  takes it.
- *Plays: quality over quantity. Punishes an opponent who massed.*

## The shape of it

|  | commander | era | plays | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cyrus | Achaemenid | wide | victory |
| 2 | Darius | Achaemenid | streaks | victory |
| 3 | Surena | Parthian | ranged attrition | victory |
| 4 | Shapur I | Sasanian | grind and convert | victory |
| 5 | Rostam Farrokhzad | Sasanian | comeback | **defeat** |
| 6 | Babak Khorramdin | 9th century | persistence | **defeat** |
| 7 | Yaqub ibn al-Layth | 9th century | cheap troops | **defeat** |
| 8 | Shah Ismail I | Safavid | all-in charge | **defeat** |
| 9 | Nader Shah | Afsharid | outnumbered speed | victory |

Six eras. **Four of the nine end in defeat** — and that is the roster telling the
truth about Iranian history rather than a marketing problem. Cyrus, Darius,
Surena, Shapur and Nader won. Rostam, Babak, Yaqub and Ismail did not, and three
of those four lost to the same expanding power in different centuries.

No two kits want the same match. Cyrus wants breadth, Darius wants a lead,
Surena wants distance, Shapur wants length, Rostam wants to be losing, Babak
wants his squads to survive, Yaqub wants the bottom of the ladder, Ismail wants
round one, Nader wants to be outnumbered.

## The two campaigns that are new

**Babak — the Qaradagh, in four stages.** *Ready to build: it needs no unit type
the game does not already have.*

1. **The mountain road** — an Abbasid column enters broken ground. Short match,
   `winsNeeded: 2`. Teaches that terrain is a weapon before it teaches anything
   about Babak.
2. **Winter at Badhdh** — a survive-objective. You do not need to win rounds, you
   need to still be standing at round seven. The only stage in the game where
   the win condition is time.
3. **The armies that came** — full first-to-4. Each round the rival's line is
   larger than the last, and **Twenty Years** is the only thing that keeps pace.
4. **Afshin** — the defeat, and the reason this campaign is worth building.
   Afshin was an Iranian prince of Ushrusana commanding for the Caliphate, and
   he beat Babak by learning the ground rather than by having more men. The
   codex says plainly that an Iranian defeated an Iranian, because a version
   that hides it is teaching something false. **Babak's execution is not
   depicted.** The entry states that he was captured and killed and stops there.

**Yaqub — Sistan to the Tigris, in four stages.**

1. **The ayyaran** — you start with nothing but Levy cards. Teaches the kit by
   removing every alternative.
2. **Sistan** — first-to-3 against a governor's proper army. Cheap troops against
   good ones, which is the whole thesis.
3. **The court at Nishapur** — first-to-4. The codex entry here carries the
   Persian-at-court story; the battle is an ordinary one and should be, because
   the interesting thing about this stage is not the fighting.
4. **Dayr al-Aqul** — the defeat, 876 CE. He got within striking distance of
   Baghdad and lost, reportedly with the Tigris let loose across his line. A
   flood is terrain, and terrain the game already models.

## What still needs deciding

1. **Ismail needs `musketeer` and `artillery`**, which exist as types but have no
   data. His campaign cannot ship before those units do — and his whole teaching
   point is losing to gunpowder, so there is no shipping him without them.
2. **Babak's stage 2 needs the survive-objective to work at `winsNeeded: 0`.**
   Two missions already use survive objectives, so this is a check, not a build.
3. **Balance is unmeasured, and the ninth commander made that worse.** Two
   commanders took a 200-match sweep to settle. **Nine is 36 pairings**, and the
   harness plays both sides of every pair — that side-advantage bug cost a whole
   afternoon of wrong readings — so it is 72 sweeps. This comes after the kits,
   not before.
4. **Nothing here is built.** Nine kits, nine portraits and roughly 32 campaign
   stages are a design, and the only commanders in the code are Cyrus and
   Astyages.
