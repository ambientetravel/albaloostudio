# The roster — eight commanders, eight ways to play

Decide this before art. Eight portraits either way.

**Honesty label, same as §10 claim 5.** The HISTORY below is sourced. The KITS
are gameplay abstractions built from that history — no Persian army ever had a
mechanic called "The Arrow Train". They are labelled reconstructed wherever they
appear in the Scrolls, exactly as the Upgrade and Doctrine names already are.

---

### 1 · CYRUS THE GREAT — the Median war · c. 550 BCE · **BUILT**
Won because other peoples were willing to fight alongside him, then did not
punish them afterwards.
- **Many Peoples** — every DIFFERENT kind of squad makes the whole line hold
  longer. Four of the same does nothing.
- **The Line Reforms** — your first lost squad gets back up once, at half.
- *Plays: wide. Rewards a mixed army.*

### 2 · DARIUS THE GREAT — the year of rebellions · 522–520 BCE
Nine kings and nineteen battles in one year, then carved his account of it into
a cliff in three languages, sixty metres up where nobody could reach it.
- **One After Another** — each round you WIN, your next squad arrives one rank
  higher. Losing resets it.
- **Cut Into The Cliff** — lock your current score advantage: the next round
  cannot be lost, only won or drawn.
- *Plays: streaks. Terrifying ahead, ordinary behind. The mirror of Cyrus.*

### 3 · SURENA — Carrhae · 53 BCE
Destroyed a Roman consular army with horse-archers and a camel train carrying
spare arrows, using feigned retreats. Executed by his own king within the year.
- **The Arrow Train** — your archers and horse-archers do not fall off; they hit
  harder the longer a round runs.
- **Feigned Flight** — your whole line withdraws and keeps shooting for a few
  seconds. Melee that chases is punished.
- *Plays: ranged attrition. Never wants to close. Historically exact.*

### 4 · ARDASHIR I — Hormozdgan · 224 CE
A vassal king kills the last Parthian ruler and four centuries of Sasanian rule
begin. The single-combat detail is later Sasanian myth-making — flagged, not
asserted.
- **The King Fights** — your commander figure on the field is far stronger than
  anyone else's.
- **Single Combat** — challenge the enemy commander directly. Both are removed
  from the line while it resolves.
- *Plays: the hero mode. The only kit built around the commander himself.*

### 5 · SHAPUR I — three Roman emperors · 244–260 CE
Misiche, Barbalissos, and Edessa where the emperor Valerian was taken alive. All
three are attested by Shapur's OWN trilingual inscription. His Roman captives
built the bridge at Shushtar that still stands.
- **Roman Hands** — every enemy squad you break leaves something behind that
  strengthens your line.
- **The Bridge at Shushtar** — spend what you have taken: one squad is rebuilt
  to full.
- *Plays: grind and convert. Rewards a long, attritional match.*

### 6 · ROSTAM FARROKHZAD — Qadisiyya · c. 636 CE · **A DEFEAT**
Four days on the Euphrates plain with the larger army, and the empire ended
anyway. Sources conflict sharply; the Arabic accounts are written later by the
winners.
- **The Fourth Day** — the further behind you are, the harder your line fights.
- **The Elephants** — deploy elephants that panic enemy cavalry outright.
- *Plays: the comeback kit. Best when losing, which is the point.*

### 7 · SHAH ISMAIL I — Chaldoran · 1514 CE · **A DEFEAT**
Safavid cavalry charged Ottoman artillery and janissary muskets. He was wounded,
lost, and never led an army again.
- **The Charge** — your cavalry hit far harder on the FIRST clash of a round,
  and no harder after it.
- **Qizilbash** — every cavalry squad advances at once, immediately.
- **Weakness, deliberately:** musketeers and artillery hurt him more than anyone.
- *Plays: all-in, first round. Teaches its own lesson by losing to gunpowder.*

### 8 · NADER SHAH — Karnal · 1739 CE
Beat a far larger Mughal army in about three hours and opened Delhi. Carried the
Peacock Throne home. Assassinated by his own officers eight years later.
- **Fewer and Faster** — while you have FEWER squads than the enemy, your whole
  line acts faster.
- **Three Hours** — end the round immediately; whoever leads on remaining health
  takes it.
- *Plays: quality over quantity. Punishes an opponent who massed.*

## The shape of it

|  | commander | era | plays | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cyrus | Achaemenid | wide | victory |
| 2 | Darius | Achaemenid | streaks | victory |
| 3 | Surena | Parthian | ranged attrition | victory |
| 4 | Ardashir I | Sasanian | hero duel | victory |
| 5 | Shapur I | Sasanian | grind and convert | victory |
| 6 | Rostam Farrokhzad | Sasanian | comeback | **defeat** |
| 7 | Shah Ismail I | Safavid | all-in charge | **defeat** |
| 8 | Nader Shah | Afsharid | outnumbered speed | victory |

No two kits want the same match. Cyrus wants breadth, Darius wants a lead,
Surena wants distance, Ardashir wants a duel, Shapur wants length, Rostam wants
to be losing, Ismail wants round one, Nader wants to be outnumbered.

## What still needs deciding

1. **Is eight right, or is six enough for launch?** Six is 25% less art and still
   covers every era. Cyrus, Surena, Shapur, Rostam, Ismail, Nader would do it —
   dropping Darius and Ardashir, both of whom duplicate a neighbour's era.
2. **Ardashir's kit depends on the commander standing on the field**, which is
   not built. If that slips, he has no kit.
3. **Ismail needs `musketeer` and `artillery`**, which exist as types but have no
   data. His campaign cannot ship before those units do.
4. **Balance is unmeasured.** Two commanders took a 200-match sweep to settle.
   Eight is 28 pairings. That is a real piece of work and it comes after the
   kits, not before.
