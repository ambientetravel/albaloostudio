# انجمن فرزانگان — the Anjoman

*Brainstorm, not decided. The commander layer is the war; this is everything else
Iran did, and it is arguably the better half.*

## 1. The name, since that was the ask

**«مدرسه» is Arabic** — from د-ر-س, to study. So are maktab, dāral-fonun and
bayt al-hekma. Ruling those out leaves real options:

| candidate | verdict |
|---|---|
| **انجمن فرزانگان** *Anjoman-e Farzānegān* — "the gathering of the wise" | **use this** |
| گندی‌شاپور *Gondēshāpur* | real Sasanian academy, but see below |
| دانشگاه *Dāneshgāh* | pure Persian, but a 1930s coinage — reads as "university" |
| خانهٔ خرد *Khāne-ye Kherad* | every word Persian, but it is a calque of bayt al-hekma |

**انجمن** is Old Persian (*hanjamana*, an assembly — it is on Achaemenid
inscriptions). **فرزانه** is pure Persian for a sage. Both are words an Iranian
child already knows, and neither has an Arabic root.

The reason to prefer it over Gondeshapur — which was a *real* Persian academy,
Sasanian, in Khuzestan, and did genuinely gather Greek, Indian and Persian
learning — is that Gondeshapur is a **place with dates**. Putting Biruni in it is
a false claim; he was born three centuries after it declined. An *anjoman* is a
gathering, not a building, and a gathering can be across time without lying.

UI word: **the Anjoman**. Full name in the codex.

## 2. The anachronism problem, and the fix that makes it an asset

You asked for Cyrus to meet Biruni. **1,523 years separate them.** The game's
whole credibility is "never invent a date, an outcome, a unit or a king" — and a
parent who catches one fabricated meeting stops trusting the honest 95%.

**The fix is a framing, and it costs nothing.** The commander never visits the
Anjoman. **The player does.**

> You are not Cyrus. You are someone in 2026 replaying his war. When you read
> Biruni and then go back to Pasargadae, *you* know how to measure a distance —
> and your line fights like someone who knows.

Nothing false is ever asserted. No "Cyrus met Biruni" screen exists. And it
quietly answers a question the game already begs: *why is a child in Toronto
choosing units for an Achaemenid king?* Because they are studying him.

This also makes the learning **load-bearing rather than decorative**. In most
educational games the fact is a sticker on top of the mechanic. Here the fact IS
the mechanic, because the power is shaped like the person's actual work.

## 3. The giants, and powers derived from what they actually did

The rule: **if the power could belong to any of them, it is wrong.** A generic
+10% is a wasted seat.

| | who | real work | the power it becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **بیرونی** | Abu Rayhan Biruni · 973 Kath, Khwarazm → 1048 Ghazna | measured the Earth's radius from one mountain observation, within ~1% | **Measurement** — see the rival's next offer, and exact numbers before you commit. He measured what everyone else guessed. |
| **ابن‌سینا** | Ibn Sina / Avicenna · 980 near Bukhara → 1037 Hamadan, buried there | the *Qānun*, a standard medical text in Europe into the 1600s | **Recovery** — once a match, a squad that would be destroyed survives instead. |
| **خیام** | Omar Khayyam · 1048 Nishapur → 1131 Nishapur | the Jalali calendar, 1079 — more accurate than the Gregorian, and still the basis of the Iranian year | **Time** — take an eighth round, or reorder the sequence. |
| **حافظ** | Hafez · c. 1315 Shiraz → c. 1390 Shiraz | the *Divān*; the **فال حافظ** is still opened for guidance in Iranian homes | **The fāl** — a free reroll, and it shows you the line of verse it landed on. |
| **فردوسی** | Ferdowsi · c. 940 Tus → c. 1020 Tus | the *Shāhnāmeh*; kept Persian alive as a literary language | **The Return** — bring back one squad you have already lost. Literally what he did. |
| **رازی** | Razi / Rhazes · 854 Rey → 925 Rey | turned alchemy into chemistry; first clinical account separating smallpox from measles | **Transmutation** — turn one card into a different card. |
| **خوارزمی** | Al-Khwarizmi · c. 780 Khwarazm → c. 850 | *algorithm* is his name; *algebra* is his book's title | **The Algorithm** — a rule you set once that then runs itself each round. |
| **طوسی** | Nasir al-Din Tusi · 1201 Tus → 1274 | built the Maragheh observatory, 1259 | **The Observatory** — see two rounds ahead instead of one. |
| **سعدی** | Saadi · c. 1210 Shiraz → c. 1291 Shiraz | the *Golestān*; «بنی‌آدم اعضای یکدیگرند» is displayed at the UN on a gifted carpet | **Mercy** — an enemy squad changes sides instead of dying. |
| **مولانا** | Rumi · 1207 Balkh → 1273 Konya | the *Masnavi* | **Union** — merge two weak squads into one strong one. |
| **جهان ملک خاتون** | Jahan Malek Khatun · 14th c. Shiraz | a princess whose *divān* of ~1,400 poems survives — a contemporary of Hafez | **The Unheard** — a card the rival believes they countered still lands. |
| **مهستی گنجوی** | Mahsati Ganjavi · 12th c. Ganja | rubāʿiyāt; wrote about working people by trade | **The Trades** — your Levy cards cost less. Pairs directly with Yaqub. |

### Three things this table forces us to be honest about

1. **A roster of nine male commanders plus twelve male sages is a problem** for a
   9–14 audience that is half girls. Jahan Malek Khatun and Mahsati are the two
   strongest genuinely attested options and both are in above, not as tokens —
   Jahan's divān survives, which is more than we have for several of the men's
   personal lives. **Parvin Etesami** (1907 Tabriz → 1941 Tehran) is a third if
   we allow the modern era.
2. **Half of these people were born outside modern Iran.** Biruni in Khwarazm and
   Avicenna near Bukhara are Uzbekistan; Rumi was born in Balkh (Afghanistan) and
   is buried in Konya (Turkey), and all three countries claim him. **Say all of
   it.** They belong to a Persianate world larger than any modern border, and
   that is a bigger claim than a nationalist one, not a smaller one.
3. **Several of them wrote in Arabic**, because it was the scholarly language —
   Biruni, Avicenna and Khwarizmi mostly did. Ferdowsi and Hafez did not. That
   tension is *already in the game*: **Yaqub ibn al-Layth is on the commander
   roster precisely for putting Persian back at court.** The war layer and the
   Anjoman layer argue with each other, truthfully, without either being wrong.

### The card each giant gets
Born · died · buried · one line of what they did · **one line of what it means
now** — Khayyam's calendar is why Nowruz falls when it does; Biruni's method is
how you measure a planet you cannot leave; the *Qānun* was on European medical
shelves for six centuries. That last line is what makes it stick.

## 4. Festivals — the calendar is the live-ops engine

Every Iranian festival is **computable**, which matters more than it sounds:
the client can derive them with no server, no campaign tooling and nobody
on call.

| festival | date | the event it obviously is |
|---|---|---|
| **نوروز** Nowruz | vernal equinox, ~20–21 Mar | the year's flagship. Renewal — everything resets, a new campaign opens |
| **چهارشنبه‌سوری** Chaharshanbe Suri | eve of the last Wednesday before Nowruz | fire. One night only, fire units and a burning arena |
| **سیزده‌بدر** Sizdah Bedar | 13 Farvardin, ~2 Apr | outdoors — every battle moves to open ground |
| **تیرگان** Tirgan | 13 Tir, ~2 Jul | **the best fit in the list.** Arash the Archer shot one arrow to set Iran's border and died doing it. An archery event that ends with a single shot |
| **مهرگان** Mehregan | 16 Mehr, ~2 Oct | Mithra, the covenant — a pact/alliance event |
| **سده** Sadeh | 10 Bahman, ~30 Jan | fire again, 50 days before Nowruz — the countdown starts here |
| **شب یلدا** Yalda | winter solstice, ~21 Dec | the longest night. Hafez's fāl is the actual tradition — his power is free that night |

**Arash must be labelled legend**, exactly as Rostam son of Zal is. He is
Avestan, not historical, and the game already has the machinery for saying so.

**The loop that closes itself:** Khayyam's power is *time*, his real achievement
is the calendar, and his calendar is what tells the game when Nowruz is. The
mechanic, the fact and the live-ops engine are the same thing.

Your month of run-up is right and is how these actually work: Sadeh is literally
50 days before Nowruz and was *already* a countdown three thousand years ago. We
did not invent the ramp; we inherited it.

## 5. What blocks it, plainly

**"Leaves a mark" needs an account, and there is no account.** Persistence today
is `localStorage` under `persia-at-war/recordings/v1` — one key, one browser.
Clear site data and a Nowruz 2027 badge is gone. The festival *trigger* is free
because it is arithmetic; the permanent *record* is not.

**Order that actually works:** ship the Anjoman with no time limits first — the
giants and their powers are valuable with zero live-ops. Add festivals as
client-computed cosmetics. Only build accounts when there is something worth
losing.

## 6. Monetisation, and the line

Do not sell knowledge. An educational game for children that gates Avicenna
behind a payment has broken its own promise, and the first review will say so.
**Every giant, every power, every codex card: free, forever.**

What is genuinely sellable is the *proof you were there*: a Nowruz 1406 frame,
a Yalda board, an Anjoman that visibly fills as you read. Cosmetic, permanent,
worthless to a cheater and priceless to a kid — and it is exactly the "leaves a
mark" you described. That is the same instinct, aimed somewhere it costs nobody
anything.
