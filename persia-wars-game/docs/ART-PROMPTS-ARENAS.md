# Arena prompts — the remaining 11

Anshan and Ecbatana already exist (`.png` + `.mp4` each); Anshan sets the
convention. These eleven match it.

Ecbatana was in the first version of this file by mistake — it is built. The
bridge inventory seed caught it, which is precisely what that seed is for. Save each as `public/art/arenas/<slug>.png` — the loader
already probes for that path, so art drops in with no code change. An optional
`<slug>.mp4` plays as the pre-battle intro.

Every prompt below is complete. Do not trim the first three paragraphs.

## The convention, read off the Anshan piece

Portrait, roughly 9:16. An isometric bird's-eye view of the real place, with a
RAISED SQUARE FIGHTING PLATFORM at the centre — lapis-blue tiled sides, gold
trim and stairs, a patterned floor with a central medallion, two small figures
duelling on it, and banner poles at its corners carrying one blue and two red
flags. The setting fills the rest of the frame. A gold border frames the whole
image, with a strip of cuneiform-like glyphs along the top and bottom edges and
a lion figure in each corner. The arena's name sits in the bottom-right corner
in small caps.

## Accuracy notes that matter

- **Gandara: no Buddhist stupas.** Gandharan Buddhist architecture is centuries
  later than 518 BCE and putting it here would be the most visible anachronism
  in the set.
- **Persepolis is under construction.** It begins around 518 BCE and is still
  being built for generations. Scaffolding and unfinished columns are correct.
- **Pasargadae is not a fortress.** Gardens and scattered pavilions, deliberately
  open. Getting this wrong contradicts the codex entry.
- **Ecbatana's seven coloured walls come from Herodotus alone** and are disputed.
  They are drawn here as a reconstruction, and the codex says so.
- **The Royal Road is not a city.** It is a relay station on an open road, and
  its composition should differ from the twelve urban views.
