# v0.3 — the commander turn

Status: **agreed in design, not built.** Written down because it decides which
art gets commissioned, and commissioning is live.

## What changes, in one line

The commander stops being a passive on a scoreboard and becomes the thing you
own, carry, level and buy — and the thing that decides which battles you may
fight at all.

## The three layers, and why they are three

Each layer has a DIFFERENT relationship to truth, and that is the teaching, not
a compromise between history and story.

| Layer | Is | Source | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Commanders** | who was actually there | the record | 8 at launch |
| **Units** | what we know they fought with | Herodotus, reliefs, coinage | 25, built |
| **Spells** | what Iranians have told about themselves | Shahnameh, Avesta | 16, written |

A child learns that Rakhsh and the Persepolis reliefs are both real things but
not the same KIND of real. Almost nothing teaches that, and this teaches it
through Iranian material rather than Greek.

`spells.json` already carries a `source` field per card — Avesta, Shahnameh, or
Iranian tradition — and the collection screen already says in plain words that
they are legend and not record. The separation exists; it just is not playable.

**Guardrail: a spell must never be the win condition.** If myth decides battles,
myth beats history mechanically and the lesson inverts. One or two uses a match,
dramatic and memorable, never decisive.

## The commander gates the battle

You fight the battles that commander actually fought. Cyrus opens Pasargadae.
Shah Ismail opens Chaldoran. Nader Shah opens Karnal.

Four jobs from one rule:

1. It gives the player a hero to carry, which is the retention spine this game
   measurably lacks.
2. Collecting commanders unlocks CONTENT, which is the only monetisation this
   audience can carry.
3. It solves navigation. Across twenty-five battles and two millennia, the
   commander IS the menu.
4. **It kills anachronism structurally rather than by rule.** Reza Shah cannot
   appear at Chaldoran because he has no battle there. Nobody has to police it.

The commander also stands ON the field — a figure with real numbers and a
signature ability — so you meet Nader Shah before you read about him. Today he
would be invisible: a passive and an order, felt but never seen.

## Deep, not wide: 9 commanders x 3-4 battles

The alternative was twenty commanders with one battle each. Rejected:

- **Attachment is what makes people spend, and nobody gets attached to twenty
  things.** Variety drives installs; identity drives retention.
- **Money follows sunk investment, not novelty.** Nobody pays to start something.
- **We measured that only 10 matches in 60 reach round 7.** Sessions are short;
  short sessions plus shallow attachment is churn.
- Nine commanders is roughly 55% less launch art than twenty, and the art queue
  is already 36 pieces behind.
- A child who plays four battles as Shah Ismail learns who he was. One battle is
  a name on a card.

8 x 3-4 still yields **25-30 battles** — the number originally wanted, reached
the other way. And arenas stay a separate ladder (§1.10), so PvP keeps its
thirteen rungs whatever the roster does. New commanders arrive as seasons.

## The cost of the ninth

Balance is pairwise, so it does not grow with the roster — it grows with the
SQUARE of it. **Eight commanders is 28 mirror-free pairings; nine is 36.** That
is 8 more matchups to sweep, and the escalation harness already runs both sides
of every pair to keep side advantage out of the reading, so it is 16 more runs
per sweep, not 8.

Accepted deliberately: the alternative was shipping a roster that jumps from
636 CE to 1501 CE with nothing in between, which is the exact stretch where
Iranian history is most often told as an absence.

## What is NOT changing

Seven rounds, first to four. No formation — the draft is the game. Simultaneous
picks. The evidence layer. The Achaemenid palette.

## Monetisation position

**Sell content, never power.** A commander unlock is his campaign, his era's
units, his myth cards — a chapter, not a weapon. That price point survives the
kids-category scrutiny that selling combat advantage would not, and it is the
only model consistent with a product whose pitch is that it is honest with
children.

The free return-reason is **mastery**: "14 battles won as Shah Ismail", a title,
a campaign at 3 of 4. Cannot be bought.

Ruled out, extending §17's ban on loot boxes and subscriptions: timers paid to
skip, streaks that punish a missed day, limited-time FOMO events. All three
manufacture compulsion in children rather than attachment, and all three would
be the first thing a reviewer names.

## What this changes in the art queue, effective immediately

Commander art moves from an afterthought (0/2) to **the launch-critical set**.
Nine commander portraits outrank the 14 mission images and the 5 capital
pieces. Unit and card art is done or nearly; arenas continue as the ladder.
