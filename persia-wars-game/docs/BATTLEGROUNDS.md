# The thirteen battlegrounds

The arena ladder, cross-checked against Encyclopædia Iranica on 12 Aug 2026.
This file is the brief for commissioning the thirteen panels: each entry has the
name, its anchor date, the modern location, what it is, an art direction note,
what is contested about it, and the reference links.

## How the order works

These are **places, not events**, so most of them existed at the same time. The
ladder is ordered by the moment each place enters the empire's story — the date
it becomes Persian. A test (`src/content/content.test.ts`) fails the build if
the sequence ever goes backwards in time.

Two corrections were made against the first draft:

1. **Susa was placed third, before Ecbatana.** That is backwards. Ecbatana falls
   in 550 BCE, the campaign that founds the empire; Susa becomes a *Persian*
   royal city only under Darius, from 521 BCE. Susa the Elamite city is far
   older than either, which is exactly why ordering by "when it became Persian"
   is the only defensible rule here.
2. **Bisotun was anchored to 520 BCE**, the year the inscription was finished,
   which put it after Susa. Its real entry point is Darius's accession in 522
   BCE — he takes the throne, then carves the account, then rebuilds Susa.

## A caveat worth keeping

and the Nabonidus Chronicle. Someone with a browser should open the Iranica

### 1. Anshan
**559 BCE** · Tal-e Malyan, Fars · unlocks at 0 trophies
*Heart of Elam, cradle of kings*

An Elamite highland capital long before it was Persian. Cyrus inherited it as a small kingdom answering to the Medes — and did not stay small.

**Art direction —** Mud-brick city on a broad plain ringed by dry Zagros ridges, irrigation channels, a stepped temple platform. Elamite rather than Persian: heavier, older, pre-column architecture. Dawn light.

**Disputed —** Anshan's identification with the mound at Tal-e Malyan was only established in the 1970s, from inscribed bricks found there. Older books place Anshan somewhere else entirely.

**Sources**
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/anshan-elamite-region/
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/malian/
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/elam-i/

### 2. Ecbatana
**550 BCE** · Hamadan · unlocks at 100 trophies
*The Median capital falls*

Astyages marched out to punish a rebellious vassal and his own army handed him over. Cyrus took the city and its treasury, and an empire started.

**Art direction —** Fortified hill city on the eastern slope of Alvand, snow on the peaks behind. Concentric walls — Herodotus describes seven, in different colours. Cold high-altitude light.

**Disputed —** Herodotus' seven concentric coloured walls are famous and unconfirmed by excavation. Treat them as a story about Ecbatana, not a plan of it.

**Sources**
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ecbatana/
- https://www.livius.org/sources/content/mesopotamian-chronicles-content/abc-7-nabonidus-chronicle/

### 3. Sardis
**547/546 BCE** · Sart, western Türkiye · unlocks at 250 trophies
*Lydia and the western frontier*

Croesus was the richest king in the world and it did not save him. Sardis became the empire's window onto the Greek sea, and the far end of the Royal Road.

**Art direction —** Acropolis on a steep spur above a river valley, Greek-influenced stonework mixed with Anatolian. Gold-washing streams below — Lydia minted the first coins. Warm western light.

**Disputed —** The date is given as 547 or 546 BCE depending on how the Nabonidus Chronicle's damaged line is restored.

**Sources**
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/capital-cities/
- https://www.britannica.com/event/Siege-of-Sardis-546-BCE
- https://www.livius.org/articles/place/sardes/

### 4. Bactra
**c. 545 BCE** · Balkh, northern Afghanistan · unlocks at 450 trophies
*The eastern marches*

The rich north-east came in without a war — Iranica reads it as a union of crowns rather than a conquest. It stayed the empire's most independent-minded province.

**Art direction —** Oasis city in irrigated green against grey desert, Central Asian mud-brick with high walls. Horses and Bactrian camels. Big empty sky, dust on the horizon.

**Disputed —** There is no firm date. Bactria is annexed under Cyrus somewhere in the 540s, and Iranica notes the annexation looks like a personal union of crowns rather than a conquest.

**Sources**
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bactria/bactria-i-pre-islamic-period/
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/achaemenid-satrapies/

### 5. Babylon
**October 539 BCE** · Hillah, Iraq · unlocks at 700 trophies
*Taken without a siege*

The greatest city on earth opened its gates. The Cyrus Cylinder, written afterwards, describes the temples he restored — in the voice of a Babylonian king.

**Art direction —** Blue-glazed brick, processional way, the Ishtar Gate's animals in relief. Flat river country, palms, the Euphrates. This is the one place that is already grander than Persia.

**Disputed —** The Cyrus Cylinder is often called the first charter of human rights. Specialists reject that: it follows a long Babylonian tradition of temple-restoration texts and is addressed to the gods, not to humanity.

**Sources**
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cyrus-iii/
- https://www.livius.org/articles/person/cyrus-the-great/
- https://peabody.yale.edu/news/finkel_talk

### 6. Pasargadae
**c. 546–530 BCE** · Dasht-e Morghab, Fars · unlocks at 1000 trophies
*Cyrus builds, and is buried*

A capital of gardens and scattered pavilions rather than one fortress, laid out where Cyrus had beaten the Medes. His tomb still stands on the plain.

**Art direction —** Open watered garden-plain 1,900 m up, isolated stone pavilions rather than a dense city, long straight water channels. The plain gabled tomb on its stepped plinth. Green against pale stone.

**Disputed —** That Pasargadae marks the site of the victory over Astyages comes from Strabo, writing five centuries later.

**Sources**
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/pasargadae/
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cyrus-v-tomb/
- https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1106/

### 7. Memphis
**525 BCE** · Mit Rahina, Egypt · unlocks at 1350 trophies
*The Great King becomes pharaoh*

Cambyses beat the Egyptians at Pelusium in spring and had the whole country by summer. That August he was installed as king of Upper and Lower Egypt.

**Art direction —** Nile-side temple precinct, colossi, palms, Egyptian colour and column forms — deliberately not Persian. Persian guards small against Egyptian scale. Hard midday sun.

**Disputed —** Herodotus' portrait of Cambyses as a madman who mocked Egyptian religion is contradicted by Egyptian sources such as the Udjahorresnet inscription, which shows him observing local protocol.

**Sources**
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cambyses-opers/
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/herodotus-v/

### 8. Bisotun
**522–520 BCE** · Kermanshah · unlocks at 1750 trophies
*Darius carves his account*

Darius took the throne in a year of rebellion and then cut his version of it into a cliff, in three languages, sixty metres up where nobody could reach it to argue.

**Art direction —** Sheer limestone cliff over the Baghdad–Ecbatana road, relief of the king with bound rebels, trilingual text panels. The scale gag is the point: enormous, unreachable, unavoidable. Late afternoon shadow.

**Disputed —** The inscription is Darius's own account of a contested succession, written to justify it. It is a primary source for what he wanted believed, not a neutral record.

**Sources**
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bisotun-iii/
- https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1222
- https://www.livius.org/articles/place/behistun/

### 9. Susa
**from 521 BCE** · Shush, Khuzestan · unlocks at 2200 trophies
*Where the empire was actually run*

An Elamite city older than Persia itself, which Darius rebuilt into the administrative capital. The paperwork of an empire ran through here, not through Persepolis.

**Art direction —** Mounded old city on the Khuzestan plain, Darius's palace and apadana, glazed-brick guard friezes in yellow and green. Hot, low, riverine — the opposite of the highland capitals.

**Disputed —** Susa entered the empire under Cyrus around 539 BCE; it becomes a Persian royal city only with Darius's building programme, which is the date used here.

**Sources**
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/susa-iii-the-achaemenid-period/
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/susa-parent/
- https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1455/

### 10. Gandara
**c. 518 BCE** · Peshawar valley, Pakistan · unlocks at 2700 trophies
*The road to the Indus*

The empire's eastern limit and, by Herodotus' reckoning, its richest tribute district. Its people appear on the Persepolis stairs carrying gifts.

**Art direction —** Green river valley under high mountains, monsoon-side vegetation unlike anywhere else in the empire. Cotton, cane, elephants. Cooler, wetter light than the plateau.

**Disputed —** The date of Darius's Indus campaign is inferred rather than recorded, and the extent of Achaemenid control east of the Indus is debated.

**Sources**
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/india-iii-relations-achaemenid-period/
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/achaemenid-satrapies/

### 11. Persepolis
**from c. 518 BCE** · Marvdasht, Fars · unlocks at 3250 trophies
*The empire performs itself*

Built for ceremony, not defence. Its stairways show every people of the empire bringing gifts — the only surviving picture of what the Persians thought their empire was.

**Art direction —** Vast terrace against the Mountain of Mercy, columned halls with double-bull capitals, the tribute-bearer stairway. Gold and lapis paint on the stone — it was not bare. Ceremonial dawn.

**Disputed —** The tribute reliefs are an official self-portrait of the empire. What they show is what the court wanted shown, not a census of who actually paid what.

**Sources**
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/persepolis/
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/apadana/
- https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/114/

### 12. The Royal Road
**Darius, from c. 500 BCE** · Sardis to Susa, ~2,400 km · unlocks at 3850 trophies
*The arteries of the empire*

A relay of riding stations every 25 to 30 km, with fresh horses waiting. Herodotus says nothing on earth travelled faster than the king's messengers.

**Art direction —** Not a city — a landscape. A stone-marked road crossing empty country, a waystation with stabled horses, a courier changing mounts. Distance and dust; the empire as logistics.

**Disputed —** The road's exact route is reconstructed from Herodotus' station counts and archaeology, and stretches of it are still argued over.

**Sources**
- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/commerce-ii/
- https://www.livius.org/articles/concept/royal-road/

### 13. The Persian Gates
**January 330 BCE** · Tang-e Meyran, near Yasuj · unlocks at 4500 trophies
*The last stand*

Two centuries after Anshan, a satrap held a Zagros gorge against Alexander with a fraction of his numbers — until someone showed the Macedonians a path over the mountain.

**Art direction —** Narrow snow-dusted defile, sheer rock either side, a river below, winter. Claustrophobic after twelve open landscapes — the empire's world closing to the width of one pass.

**Disputed —** Arrian's 40,000 defenders are regarded as grossly inflated; Iranica suggests as few as 700. The shepherd who reveals the mountain path mirrors Herodotus' Ephialtes at Thermopylae closely enough to be doubted.

**Sources**
- https://www.livius.org/articles/battle/persian-gate-330-bce/
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Persian_Gate
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariobarzanes,_Satrap_of_Persis
