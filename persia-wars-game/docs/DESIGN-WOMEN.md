# The women — attested, legendary, and invented

*Brainstorm. Companion to `DESIGN-ANJOMAN.md` and `COMMANDER-ROSTER.md`.*

## Start here: four famous names have no ancient source

This matters more than anything else on the page, because these are the names an
Iranian child will bring to the game already knowing, and the game's whole
promise is that it does not invent.

| name | what is claimed | what exists |
|---|---|---|
| **پانته‌آ ارتشبد** Pantea Arteshbod | commander of the Immortals, wife of Aryasb | Xenophon's *Cyropaedia* has a Panthea — a **captive noblewoman of Susa**, not a soldier — and the *Cyropaedia* is a philosophical romance, not history. **"Commander of the Immortals" appears in no ancient text.** Modern. |
| **آپرانیک** Apranik | Sasanian general resisting the Arab conquest | **no primary source at all.** Not in al-Tabari, not in Sebeos, not on a coin, not in a Pahlavi text. Twentieth-century. |
| **یوتاب** Youtab | sister of Ariobarzanes, fought at the Persian Gate | Ariobarzanes is real and the Persian Gate is real. **Youtab is not in any account of it.** Modern. |
| **آرتمیس** as a Persian admiral | often merged with Artemisia | Artemisia is real (below) but was **Carian Greek**, not Persian |

**Why they spread is the interesting part, and it belongs in the game.** The gap
is real — chronicles written by men mostly did not record women — and inventing
heroes is what people do when a gap hurts. The answer is not to accept the
inventions. It is this:

> **Purandokht ruled the Sasanian Empire and there are coins with her name on
> them. Almost nobody knows who she was. Apranik has no source and is on every
> poster.**

That single comparison is the game's thesis in one line, and it is worth a codex
entry of its own.

## 1 · Commanders — the two that hold up

### آرتمیزیا — ARTEMISIA I of Halicarnassus · Salamis, 480 BCE
Queen of Halicarnassus, an Achaemenid vassal, who brought **five ships** to
Xerxes' fleet. Herodotus is the source — and he was born in Halicarnassus
himself, which is a reason to trust the detail and to watch the flattery.

**Her famous act was advice, not combat.** She told Xerxes not to fight at
Salamis: the Greeks could not hold their fleet together, wait and they scatter.
He fought. He lost. Herodotus has him say afterwards that his men had become
women and his women men.

- **She was not Persian.** Carian Greek, commanding for the Great King. The
  Achaemenid empire ran on exactly that, and saying so is better history than
  claiming her.
- **The kit writes itself, and it is unlike anything on the roster:
  a commander whose power is *not fighting*.** Her passive shows you which of
  your offers is genuinely the strongest. Her order lets you **refuse a round** —
  concede it, keep your line intact for the next. Nobody else can decline.
- **Blocker: Salamis is a sea battle and the game has no ships.** Either she
  enters on the counsel kit alone with her campaign told at anchor, or a naval
  layer gets built. A naval layer is a large piece of work — but the Persian Gulf
  and the Achaemenid fleet are sitting there for later either way.
- *No likeness. Coins of Halicarnassus at best.*

### پوران‌دخت — PURANDOKHT · Ctesiphon, 630–631 CE
Daughter of Khosrow II. **Queen regnant of the Sasanian Empire in her own name,
with coins struck bearing it** — which is harder evidence than survives for most
of the men on the commander roster. She took the throne in the wreckage after the
Byzantine war, made peace with Heraclius, and returned the True Cross to
Jerusalem. Her sister **آزرمی‌دخت Azarmidokht** ruled shortly after her.

- This is exactly the span you asked about: **the last decade before the Arab
  conquest.** Purandokht is roughly five years before Qadisiyya. She and Rostam
  Farrokhzad are the same collapse seen from the throne and from the field, and
  the campaigns can cross.
- **Kit: the empire she inherited was broken.** Something like — she begins with
  fewer squads than anyone, and her power is what she can rebuild between rounds.
  A commander who starts behind by design and whose whole game is repair.
- **Her campaign is peace, not conquest.** Winning a round can mean ending it
  early. That is a genuinely different verb for this game and it is what she
  actually did.
- *No likeness. The coins show a crowned figure but are not portraiture.*

### On the line, and worth a decision: گردیه — GORDIYA
Sister of Bahram Chobin, late 6th century. She appears in Ferdowsi **and** in
al-Tabari and Dinawari, led her brother's troops after his death, and married
Khosrow II. Better attested than Apranik, weaker than Purandokht. If the roster
takes a third woman she is the candidate — and the codex entry says plainly that
the epic and the chronicles disagree about her.

### Also real, probably out of scope
**موسا — Musa of Parthia** (c. 2 BCE – 4 CE), who ruled with her son and is on
Parthian coins. An Italian slave girl sent by Augustus to Phraates IV who ended
up on the throne. Entirely attested and entirely strange. **پادشاه خاتون —
Padeshah Khatun** (Kerman, r. 1291–95), who ruled in her own name, minted coins,
*and* wrote poetry — she could enter as either commander or sage.

## 2 · Legends — the same rule as Rostam son of Zal

These are Shahnameh figures. The game already separates the legendary Rostam
from the historical Rostam Farrokhzad, and that machinery applies unchanged.
They belong on **spell cards**, not the commander roster.

- **گردآفرید — Gordafarid.** Rides out disguised as a man, fights Sohrab to a
  standstill in single combat, and talks her way out when unmasked. One of the
  best-known warrior women in Persian literature and she wins by wits.
- **بانوگشسپ — Banu Goshasp.** Rostam's daughter, with her own epic, the
  *Banu Goshasp Nama*.
- **همای چهرزاد — Homay Chehrzad**, who ruled in the Shahnameh.

## 3 · The Anjoman — women in the gathering

Adding to the twelve already in `DESIGN-ANJOMAN.md`:

| | who | real work | power it becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **مریم میرزاخانی** | **Maryam Mirzakhani** · 1977 Tehran → 2017 | **the first woman ever to win the Fields Medal**, 2014, on the geometry of curved surfaces | **The Surface** — the arena's terrain works for you instead of against you. She studied the shape of the ground. |
| **آلنوش طریان** | Alenush Terian · 1920 Tehran → 2011 | Iranian-Armenian physicist, founded Tehran University's solar observatory — "the mother of Iranian astronomy" | **The Sun** — see one round further, but only in daylight arenas |
| **رابعه بلخی** | Rabia Balkhi · 10th c., Balkh | traditionally the first woman to write poetry in **New Persian** | **First Words** — your first card of the match costs nothing |
| **پروین اعتصامی** | Parvin Etesami · 1907 Tabriz → 1941 Tehran | debate-poems in which two ordinary things argue — a needle and a thread, a moth and a candle | **The Argument** — two of your squads swap places mid-round |
| **جهان ملک خاتون** | Jahan Malek Khatun · 14th c. Shiraz | already in the Anjoman: ~1,400 poems, divān survives | *(unchanged)* |
| **مهستی گنجوی** | Mahsati Ganjavi · 12th c. Ganja | already in the Anjoman | *(unchanged)* |

**Mirzakhani is the strongest single addition to this whole design.** For an
Iranian girl of nine to eleven in the diaspora, a woman from Tehran holding the
highest prize in mathematics is not a history lesson — it is a door. Nothing else
on either roster does that job.

**One name deliberately flagged, not included:** **طاهره قرةالعین Tahirih /
Qurrat al-Ayn**, a major 19th-century poet, was a Babi. Including her would make
the game a problem to distribute inside Iran. That is a distribution fact, not a
judgement, and it is your call rather than mine.

**And one honest gap:** there is no attested named woman scientist in Persian
before the modern period that I can put in front of a child and defend. Rabia is
a poet whose biography is largely legend. The absence is real and the codex
should say the absence is real — the alternative is inventing a Persian woman
astronomer, which is precisely what Apranik is.

## 4 · What it costs

**Balance is pairwise and grows with the square.** Nine commanders is 36
pairings; **eleven is 55**, and the harness plays both sides, so 110 sweeps.
Artemisia and Purandokht are worth it — but they are not free, and Artemisia
additionally wants a naval layer that does not exist.

**Cheapest order that still changes the game:** the Anjoman women cost nothing
structural — they are content in a system already designed. **Mirzakhani, Terian,
Rabia and Parvin can ship the day the Anjoman does.** Purandokht is next, because
her campaign reuses the Sasanian collapse already built for Rostam Farrokhzad.
Artemisia is last, because she is the one that needs ships.

# Second pass — voices, Esther, the Shahnameh women, and the modern age

## 5 · The singers, and the fact that defines them

**هایده Hayedeh** — Ma'soumeh Dadehbala, 1942 Tehran → 1990 San Francisco,
dead at 47, buried in Westwood, Los Angeles. **مهستی Mahasti** — Eftekhar
Dadehbala, 1946 → 2007, **her actual younger sister**, who died in Los Angeles
too. Two sisters, two of the largest voices in Persian music, both buried in
California.

**The fact that matters:** solo singing by women in public has been banned in
Iran since 1979. Hayedeh and Mahasti left and never sang publicly at home again.
**These are the voices that play at diaspora gatherings precisely because they
cannot be heard in Tehran** — which means for the audience this game is aimed at,
they are not history. They are the sound of the room the kid is sitting in.

Mahasti also shares her name with **مهستی گنجوی Mahasti Ganjavi**, the
twelfth-century poet already in the Anjoman. Eight hundred years apart, one name.

**Powers, shaped as always by the actual work:**
- **Hayedeh — «The Held Note».** Her range was the thing. One squad keeps
  fighting for a full extra round after it should have stopped.
- **Mahasti — «The Sister».** Any power gained by the other sister applies to
  you too. They only ever really work as a pair.

**And the one who came first, who is safer and arguably more important:**
**قمرالملوک وزیری Qamar-ol-Molouk Vaziri**, 1905–1959 — **the first Iranian
woman to sing in public without a hijab**, Grand Hotel Tehran, 1924. Everything
Hayedeh could do, she could do because Qamar did it first. Power: **«The First
Time»** — the first of anything you do in a match counts double.

**The honest flag.** Hayedeh, Mahasti and Qamar are all a statement inside Iran
in a way Ferdowsi is not. This goes in the same box as Tahirih: a distribution
question for a diaspora-first game, and your call, not mine. **مرضیه Marzieh**
(1924–2010, died in Paris) and **دلکش Delkash** (1924–2004) are equally real if
a longer list helps.

**One category flag: living people.** گوگوش Googoosh, silenced in Iran for
twenty-one years and singing again since 2000, is the biggest name here and is
alive. Living figures bring likeness rights and the possibility of later
disagreement. Everyone else on these lists is dead, which is not a small
administrative difference.

## 6 · استر — Esther

Yes, and she is a better fit than she first looks, because the game already has
the machinery.

- **The Book of Esther** places her as queen to **Ahasuerus**, which is
  Khshayarsha — **Xerxes I**. She prevents the destruction of the Jews of the
  empire. Purim commemorates it.
- **The Tomb of Esther and Mordechai in Hamadan is real and still stands** — one
  of the most important Jewish sites in Iran, and a working pilgrimage place.
- **What is not attested:** Esther appears in no Persian or Greek source.
  Herodotus names Xerxes' queen as **Amestris**. Most historians read the book as
  a novella rather than a chronicle.

**So she goes exactly where Rostam son of Zal goes** — the legend tier, with the
codex saying all three things at once: here is the story, here is the tomb that
is genuinely there, here is what no record confirms. The game does this already
and does it well.

**What it lets the game say, which is true and worth saying:** the Jews of Iran
are one of the oldest continuous Jewish communities on earth, and they are there
because **Cyrus freed them from Babylon** — and the **Cyrus Cylinder** is a real
object in the British Museum, not a story. Commander #1 already carries this.
Stated plainly, with no modern reading attached in either direction, per the rule
already on the books.

**Power: «The Petition».** She goes to the king uninvited, which could have
killed her. Once per match, ask for something you have not earned — take a card
from outside your offer.

## 7 · The Shahnameh women — the deep dive

All spell-tier, all flagged legend, same as Rostam son of Zal.

| | who she is | the spell |
|---|---|---|
| **رودابه Rudabeh** | princess of Kabul, Zal's love, Rostam's mother. Lets down her hair from the tower — and he refuses to hurt her by climbing it, and throws a rope instead. Rostam's birth is the first caesarean in the poem, on the Simurgh's advice | **The Rope** — reach a card two ranks above you |
| **تهمینه Tahmineh** | princess of Samangan. She comes to Rostam at night and asks him for a child — **she chooses**. Sohrab is the result, and Rostam kills him not knowing him | **The Token** — mark one enemy card; you cannot be made to fight it |
| **گردآفرید Gordafarid** | rides out disguised as a man, fights Sohrab to a standstill, and when unmasked talks her way back inside the walls. **She wins on wits, not strength** | **The Unmasking** — an enemy card is revealed and loses its trait |
| **بانوگشسپ Banu Goshasp** | Rostam's daughter, with her own epic, the *Banu Goshasp Nama* | **The Daughter** — inherit the traits of a squad you have lost |
| **سیندخت Sindokht** | Rudabeh's mother, who **negotiates the peace between Kabul and Iran herself** — she rides out and does the diplomacy | **The Terms** — end a round in a draw instead of a loss |
| **منیژه Manijeh** | Afrasiab's daughter. Cast out with nothing, she keeps Bijan alive in the pit by begging for food door to door | **The Pit** — a squad on 1 HP cannot be killed this round |
| **فرنگیس Farangis** | Afrasiab's daughter, Siyavash's wife, mother of Kay Khosrow — she gets her son out | **The Escape** — withdraw a squad and keep it for next round |
| **همای چهرزاد Homay** | ruled Iran in her own name for thirty-two years in the poem | **The Regency** — command with no commander for one round, and pay nothing for it |
| **سودابه Sudabeh** | falsely accuses Siyavash and destroys him. **The antagonist** | **The False Word** — an enemy trait is turned against them. *A woman who is the villain is good writing, not bad representation* |

**Two who cross the line between story and record:**
- **شیرین Shirin** — Nizami's *Khosrow and Shirin* is a romance, but **Khosrow II
  genuinely had a Christian wife named Shirin, attested in the chronicles.** She
  is legend AND history, and the game's two tiers can hold her in both at once —
  which is a nice demonstration of why the two tiers exist.
- **شهرزاد Scheherazade** — frame-narrator of the *Thousand and One Nights*,
  whose Persian core is the *Hezār Afsān*. **She survives by telling a better
  story each night.** Power: **«One More Night» — postpone a defeat by one
  round.** That is the best single female spell card available and it is exactly
  what the character does.

Also outside Ferdowsi: **ویس Vis** of *Vis o Rāmin* (Gorgani, 11th century,
older than Tristan and Isolde and possibly upstream of it), and **لیلی Layli**
of Nizami.

## 8 · Cinema and the modern age

- **فروغ فرخزاد Forough Farrokhzad** · 1934 Tehran → 1967, dead at 32. Major
  modernist poet **and a filmmaker** — *خانه سیاه است* / *The House Is Black*
  (1963), on a leprosy colony, is a foundation stone of the Iranian New Wave.
  **She belongs in the Anjoman twice over.** Power: **«The Other Wind»** — change
  the arena mid-match.
- **مرجانه ساتراپی Marjane Satrapi** · b. 1969. ***Persepolis* is about an
  Iranian girl aged roughly nine to fourteen** growing up through a revolution
  and then into a diaspora. **That is this game's player, in a book.** Living —
  see the flag below.
- **رخشان بنی‌اعتماد Rakhshan Bani-Etemad**, **سمیرا مخملباف Samira
  Makhmalbaf** (in Cannes competition at seventeen), **شیرین نشاط Shirin
  Neshat**. All living.
- **مریم میرزاخانی Maryam Mirzakhani** — already in the Anjoman and still the
  strongest addition on any of these lists.
- **کیمیا علیزاده Kimia Alizadeh** — first Iranian woman to win an Olympic medal,
  Rio 2016. Living.
- **شیرین عبادی Shirin Ebadi** (Nobel Peace, 2003) and **نرگس محمدی Narges
  Mohammadi** (Nobel Peace, 2023). Both living, both political in a way that goes
  well past Hayedeh, and Narges Mohammadi's situation is ongoing. **My
  recommendation is to leave both out of a children's game** — not because they
  are unworthy, but because the game cannot hold a live political case honestly
  at this age level, and half-holding it would be worse than not.

**The living-people rule this suggests:** everyone on the historical rosters is
dead, which is why the game can say what they did and stop. A living person can
say something tomorrow that the game is then carrying. **Default: the dead.**
Mirzakhani, Forough, Hayedeh, Mahasti, Terian, Qamar all qualify. Satrapi is the
one living exception I would argue for, and only with her permission.
