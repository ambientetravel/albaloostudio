# Bridge brief — Persia at War

Same bridge, same contract as the website manifests: the analyst writes a
proposal, it lands in git, `bridge_ingest.py` validates shape, **a reviewer
decides**. Not a live model-to-model API, and nothing auto-applies. Whatever
model sits on the far end is irrelevant to the design and always has been.

## Hand it the seed first

`inventory/persia-at-war-seed.json`, regenerated by `build_game_seed.py` before
every handoff. It is a factual map of what is built, what was measured, what was
decided and closed, and what is off-limits.

This is not optional. Three website manifests were wasted on a fabricated date,
schema already live, and content already built — every one because the model was
blind to what existed. **The very first run of this game seed caught its author
proposing an arena prompt for Ecbatana, which was already finished.** If it
catches me, it will catch anything.

## Where an outside analyst genuinely adds value

Feed it only what this project cannot see for itself.

- **Live genre retrieval.** The strongest input, and the direct analogue of
  `geo_optimization` on the website side: this codebase has a fixed knowledge
  cutoff and no browsing. What is actually working right now in the
  Clash-Royale-like and auto-battler space — progression curves, session length,
  retention hooks, what died and why — is the one surface we are blind to.
- **Adversarial critique of the loop.** Give it the round loop and ask why a
  ten-year-old stops at round three. We measured that only 10 matches in 60
  reach round 7; we have no theory of why a child would want to.
- **Educational-game conventions for 9-14.** Reading load, session length,
  what holds attention, how other history games handle uncertainty honestly.
- **Soft-launch strategy.** Markets, store category, comparable titles, ASO.
- **Retention.** There is no daily loop here worth the name.

## What must not be routed here

- **Balance numbers.** Measured with harnesses and 248 tests. An opinion about
  `TIER_MULT` that has not run the sweep is noise, and the sweep is one command.
- **Any historical claim.** A general model will confidently produce the Cyrus
  Cylinder as a charter of human rights. That exact claim is named and banned in
  our own `mustNotClaim`. History goes to a historian. This is the hardest rule
  on the page.
- **Code review, refactors, test strategy, art prompts, anything needing Unity.**

## The review gate, and why it is not optional

The automated gate checks shape, sources and banned terms. It passed a manifest
in which every one of five tasks was addressed to the wrong agent. Substance
needs a reviewer.

And the failure that was mine rather than the model's: two of Gemini's answers
were forwarded as accurate and the receiving session caught them — one offered a
product not in that property's inventory, one reversed a booking flow. The rule
that came out of it applies here unchanged:

> **True is necessary but not sufficient. It must also match what we have.**

For this game that means: a proposal can be a perfectly good idea about
auto-battlers and still be wrong here, because it assumes alternating picks, or
a Unity backend, or an age gate we deliberately removed. Check every proposal
against the seed before acting, not after.

## Returning work

One file per handoff in `accepted/`, named `persia-<YYYY-MM-DD>.json`, with a
`.review.md` beside it recording what was routed, what was dropped, and why —
the same record the website manifests keep. Every task carries
`provenance.sources`. A claim with no source is a suggestion, not a directive.
